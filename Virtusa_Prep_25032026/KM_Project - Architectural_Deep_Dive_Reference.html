10 Interview Narrative — Data Volume Questions

"How much data are you handling?"

Ready-to-use Answer "At Kahramaa, we manage data from approximately 988,000 smart meters — 528,000 electricity and 460,000 water — each reading every 30 minutes, generating roughly 50 million IoT readings per day. That's approximately 18 GB of raw daily ingestion from the IoT layer alone, plus Oracle ERP billing data and CRM batch feeds. After Delta Lake columnar compression with Snappy, the persistent Bronze layer grows by about 4.5 GB per day, and the Gold star schema by under 1 GB per day. Over the 2–3 year project lifecycle, the total platform footprint across all layers is approximately 8–10 TB. The platform is architected to scale to 2 million meters at full deployment by 2027, which projects to roughly double the current throughput."

"What was your biggest data engineering challenge?"

Ready-to-use Answer "The most significant challenge was managing the small-file problem from continuous IoT ingestion. At 50 million readings per day across 988,000 meters, each Structured Streaming micro-batch wrote thousands of small Parquet files to the Bronze Delta table. Without intervention, Bronze would accumulate 20,000+ files per day, degrading read performance exponentially. I implemented a weekly OPTIMIZE pipeline on the Bronze table to compact files to the 128–512 MB target range, followed by Z-ORDER on meter_id and event_time — the two most frequent query predicates. This reduced Bronze query time from over 4 minutes to under 30 seconds for a full-day scan. I also tuned Structured Streaming trigger intervals to balance latency vs file count."

"How did you handle data quality at 50M records/day?"

Ready-to-use Answer "I implemented a five-layer validation framework in the Bronze→Silver transition. First, schema enforcement — Delta rejects any record whose schema doesn't match. Second, null checks on mandatory fields: meter_id, timestamp, and reading_value cannot be null. Third, range validation — consumption values must fall within physically plausible bounds per meter type. Fourth, duplicate detection using a window function on meter_id and timestamp for a 2-hour dedup window, since Siemens AMI can occasionally retransmit readings on network recovery. Fifth, anomaly flagging — readings 3 standard deviations above the customer's 30-day rolling average are routed to a quarantine table for manual review. Great Expectations ran as a Databricks task between Bronze and Silver loads, with results published to a data quality dashboard in Power BI."

⚡ The Bridging Statement for the ENBD Interview "My Kahramaa platform processes 50 million IoT readings daily across 988,000 smart meters with a medallion Lakehouse architecture on Azure Databricks and Delta Lake. The engineering patterns — incremental watermark extraction, columnar compression, partition-based query optimization, and dead-letter quality routing — are identical at ENBD's scale. Scaling to 800 TB total and 500 TB monthly throughput requires tuning the knobs, not redesigning the architecture: more JDBC partitions on Oracle extraction, larger executor footprints, Adaptive Query Execution enabled by default, and disciplined Z-ordering on high-cardinality columns. In a CDP/Hive environment specifically, the Delta Lake transaction log equivalent is the Hive Metastore — I would use MSCK REPAIR for partition recovery and Spark's native HiveContext to read Hive tables, which I understand well from the underlying Spark-Hive integration model."
